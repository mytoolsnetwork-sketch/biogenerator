What Is a Professional Bio?

A professional bio is a short, polished paragraph that summarises who you are, what you do, and what makes you credible — written in the third person for use on websites, speaker profiles, press kits, conference programmes, and portfolios. It's the professional world's equivalent of a first impression, and it needs to be both accurate and compelling.

Short Bio vs Long Bio — Which Do You Need?

Short professional bio (50–80 words)

Used for speaker introductions, social media profiles, email signatures, and anywhere space is limited. It should cover your role, your key credential, and one line about your mission or impact. Every word counts — cut anything that doesn't add information.

Long professional bio (150–200 words)

Used for website About pages, press kits, conference programmes, and grant applications. It can include career history, key achievements, publications or media appearances, and a personal detail that humanises you.

Professional Bio Tips

  • Write in third person — "Sarah is a..." not "I am a..." — for formal professional contexts.
  • Lead with your current role — don't bury the most relevant information.
  • Be specific about achievements — numbers and named credentials ("TEDx speaker," "Forbes 30 Under 30") carry more weight than adjectives.
  • End with a human touch — a personal detail (where you live, a hobby, your family) makes you more relatable and memorable.
  • Keep it updated — a bio with an outdated job title undermines your credibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you write a professional bio about yourself?

Start with your current role and organisation, add your years of experience and field, include your most notable achievement or credential, describe your mission or what you help people do, and close with a personal detail. Write in third person for formal use.

How long should a professional bio be?

A short bio should be 50–80 words — enough for a speaker introduction or a profile page sidebar. A full bio should be 150–200 words — long enough to establish credibility without losing the reader's attention.

Should a professional bio be written in first or third person?

Third person ("Sarah is a...") is standard for formal professional bios used on websites, speaker profiles, and press kits. First person ("I am a...") works better for personal About pages where a conversational tone is more appropriate.
