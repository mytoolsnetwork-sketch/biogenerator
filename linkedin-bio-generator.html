How to Write a Great LinkedIn Bio

Your LinkedIn bio has two parts that matter most: your headline (visible everywhere) and your summary (visible on your profile). Together they determine whether recruiters click through, whether potential clients reach out, and whether the right people find you in search.

LinkedIn Headline Best Practices

Your LinkedIn headline gets 220 characters and appears next to your name in every comment, connection request, and search result. The best headlines include your role, your key skill or specialty, and a value statement — all separated clearly so the eye can scan them quickly.

LinkedIn Summary Best Practices

  • Start with a hook — your first two lines appear before "see more," so make them count.
  • Be specific about your expertise — vague summaries get ignored. Name the tools, industries, and outcomes you deliver.
  • Include keywords — LinkedIn's search algorithm surfaces profiles that include the keywords recruiters search for.
  • End with a call to action — tell people what to do next: connect, message, or visit your website.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I write in my LinkedIn bio?

Your LinkedIn bio (summary) should cover: what you do and who you help, your key skills and experience, a notable achievement, and a clear call to action. Keep it between 150 and 300 words for maximum impact.

How long should a LinkedIn summary be?

LinkedIn allows up to 2,600 characters in the summary section, but the sweet spot is 150-300 words. Long enough to show depth, short enough to be read in full.

Should I write my LinkedIn bio in first or third person?

First person ("I help companies...") feels more personal and direct — it's the recommended approach for most profiles. Third person works better for very senior executives or public figures where a formal, press-release tone is expected.
